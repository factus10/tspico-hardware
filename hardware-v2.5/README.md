# TS-Pico v2.5 KiCad project

The v2.2 card (`../old/`, CircuitStudio, September 2023) redrawn all surface mount for JLCPCB assembly, on the v3 board outline (130 x 64.25 mm, 56 mm body plus the 8.25 mm tongue, same edge connector, 4 layers). The circuit, the designators and the Pico pin map are v2.2's, so the shipped firmware (tspico-firmware-build) runs unchanged. One block is new: bench programming of the flash from the Pico, so a board with a blank soldered flash can be loaded without a ROM programmer or a 2068.

Status 2026-10-11: schematic ERC clean and board DRC clean at every severity (no errors, no warnings, nothing unconnected); `scripts/check.sh` passes. Fully routed by `flow.py` on its first attempt with no hand edits: 2496 track segments and 406 vias (all 0.6/0.3 mm), every track on F.Cu or B.Cu, so both planes are unbroken; narrowest track 0.13 mm. Fab outputs (Gerbers, JLC BOM and CPL) are generated by `scripts/gen_fab.py`. Not built. JLC parts plus loading fees: $23.92 per board on a 10-board run, of which the Pico, flash and SRAM are $16.29.

## Bench programming the flash

The shipped firmware maps the 2068's HOME ROM from flash unconditionally at power-on (`src/dev_tspico.py`, `set_ctrl` started at line 5693), and `flash/README.md` says new boards get the 512K image burned with a programmer. A soldered blank flash would therefore leave the 2068 unable to boot, and the in-place `romupdate.tap` path needs a working ROM first. v2.2 has no free Pico GPIO to drive the flash address bus, so v2.5 adds a shift-register path that reuses existing pins:

- U12, U14, U15: 74HCT595 chain. SER = D1_L (GP3), SRCLK = D0_L (GP2) (the SPI0 pins already used for the SD card), RCLK = SD_CS (GP28). U12 drives A0-A7, U14 A8-A13, /RD and the flash /WE, U15 drives D0-D7 through RN1/RN2 (4.7 k). A14/A15 come from the Pico through U8 (R13 holds /ROSCS high off the bus), A16-A18 and /CE as in v2.2.
- Read-back for verify: U15's outputs high, /RD low, U6 enabled. R14 holds /IORQ high, so U6 points at the Pico, and the byte arrives on GP2-GP9. The 4.7 k series resistors let the flash win against U15.
- Power: SW4 (slide switch, RUN/PROG) in PROG puts the Pico's USB VBUS on the card's +5V through D3, so the memories and logic run with the card out of the computer.
- Enable: the 595 outputs are on only when SW4 is in PROG, VBUS is present, and there is no +15 V on edge pin B3 (Q2/Q3). Plugged into a powered 2068 they stay off whatever the switch says. In the 2068, SD traffic clocks garbage into the chain, but the outputs are disabled, so nothing reaches the bus.
- Procedure: card out of the 2068, SW4 to PROG, USB to the Pico, copy the 512K image to the Pico as `/flash.rom`, run `tools/benchprog.py` (draft, untested: chip erase, byte program, verify; a few minutes in MicroPython). SW4 back to RUN before the card goes into the computer.

The Z80-side `romupdate.tap` path works exactly as on v2.2 (P10 closed).

Things to keep in mind: with SW4 in PROG and USB plugged into a 2068 that is switched off, the USB port would try to power the 2068's 5 V rail (D3 conducts). The port's current limit protects the host, but keep SW4 in RUN except on the bench. The card's rail sits at about 4.7 V in PROG mode (USB minus a Schottky), which is inside the flash's 4.5-5.5 V range and fine for LS logic at bench temperatures.

## Layout

The Pico sits along the top left with its USB out of the left edge. Its GP0-GP15 row faces down toward the logic. Above it, left to right: SW4 and D3 by VBUS, D2/C2 by VSYS, then the Pico-side pull-ups. The buttons are along the top edge (SW1 NMI, SW2 TS reset, SW3 Pico reset), each with its DNP external-button pads. The SRAM and flash sit in the middle band, and the 555 reset is in the top-right corner. The micro SD is on the left edge below the Pico, its shell flush with the edge. The glue row (U1, U5, U4, U9, U8, U7) and the two address 595s (U12, U14) run across the board, with Q2/Q3 (the program-enable interlock) at its right end. U6 sits over the data fingers, and the data 595 (U15) with RN1/RN2 sits at the left end of the fingers. An earlier placement with U15 at the far end of the 595 row left one of its eight outputs unroutable twice.

Every used bottom-side finger (address, +15 V, +5 V, GND, /BE_IN) gets a locked B.Cu stub and a via 1.4 mm into the body (`gen_layout.py`); +5V gets two vias and a 0.5 mm stub into the In2 plane. Without them the router boxed in B4 (+5V) and B13 (A3).

## Before ordering

- Stock: U10 (C632847, 17 at JLC), U13 (C511135, 16), U9 (C7731, 56). Consign or swap if short; AS6C4008-55SIN fits U13's footprint and pinout.
- U13: check the CY62148E SOIC-32 pinout against the datasheet: /CE pin 22, /OE pin 24, /WE pin 29, VCC 32, GND 16. Address and data order do not matter for an SRAM only the Z80 sees.
- TF-01A footprint and card-detect: same open items as v3 (`../hardware/README.md`).
- JLC rotations for the PLCC, SOICs, Pico module and switches in the placement preview.
