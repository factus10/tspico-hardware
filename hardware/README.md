# TS-Pico v3 KiCad project

Status 2026-10-12: prototype 3 in this folder (changes below). Proto1 ([v3.0-proto1](https://github.com/factus10/tspico-hardware/releases/tag/v3.0-proto1)) was built and brought up; proto2 added its fixes (below). Both proto2 and proto3 were routed by `scripts/flow.py` with no hand edits, DRC clean at every severity. ERC: one warning, U4's unused input tied to GND.

**To order:** the JLCPCB upload files (Gerber zip, BOM, placement), ordering notes, schematic PDF and renders are on the [v3.0-proto2.1 release](https://github.com/factus10/tspico-hardware/releases/tag/v3.0-proto2.1): the proto2 board with L1 and U22 fitted by hand. [v3.0-proto2](https://github.com/factus10/tspico-hardware/releases/tag/v3.0-proto2) has the same Gerbers with L1 assembled by JLCPCB. To regenerate them from this folder, run `python3 scripts/gen_fab.py` (output in `fab/jlcpcb/`).

## Proto3 changes

| Change | Parts | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Male DE-9 | J5 CONNFLY DS1037-09MNAKT74-0CC (C75750), footprint `DSUB-9_Pins_Horizontal_…` | Atari sticks (CX-40) end in a female plug, like the 2068's own male ports expect. Proto1/2 had a female footprint; a male connector on it mirrors every pin, putting the stick's ground (pin 8) on pin 7 (JOY_5V, open) so nothing reads. Same body position and holes as proto2. On a proto1/2 board, mount a male connector on the back of the board: turning it over puts each pin on its own pad. |
| Buttons and LED moved | SW3 under SW1, SW4 under SW2; D4/R5 left of the ESP32 | A 1.54 in module on J3 covered BOOTSEL and crowded RUN. |
| I2C OLED header | J3 1x4 (1 GND, 2 +3V3, 3 SCL, 4 SDA), R44/R45 4k7 pull-ups | For a 1.54 in 128x64 SSD1309 I2C module, on I2C1 (GP43 SCL, GP42 SDA). Replaces the 7-pin header for the 1.27 in SSD1351 SPI module. GP34, GP41 and expander P0.2 (with its pull-down R11) are freed. |

## Proto2 changes (from proto1 bring-up and issue #21)

| Change | Parts | Why |
|---|---|---|
| nBE pull-up to +5V_EDGE | R14 2k2 | The 74LVC06 only pulls /BE low; without a pull-up it rose too slowly and a RAM opcode fetch after a served byte read wrong bits. Proto1 boards carry a 2.2k bodge from A29 to +5V. |
| GP8-GP10 pull-ups | R15-R17 10k | nAEN_LO, nAEN_HI, nDATA_OE: the RP2350 resets its pads with pull-downs, so in reset and BOOTSEL all three buffers were enabled and U1/U2 fought on MD. |
| SD socket power switch | U32 SY6280AAC, R42 15k (0.45 A), R43 10k, C52 1u | Socket VDD is now +3V3_SD, switched from XL9555 P0.6 (SD_PWR_EN, off at power-up). A latched card hits the current limit instead of resetting the RP2350B; the firmware can power-cycle a card. R34-R37 moved to +3V3_SD; R38 (detect) stays on +3V3. |
| U22 footprint | SOIC-8 3.9 x 4.9 mm | The APS6404L-3SQR-SN is SOP-8 150 mil (AP Memory package code SN); proto1 had the 208 mil footprint. Still DNP; see `docs/bom-cost-optimization.md` for PSRAM options. |
| L1 fitted by hand | L1 DNP at JLCPCB | The RP2350 core-regulator inductor (Abracon AOTA-B201610S3R3-101-T) was out of stock at JLCPCB and LCSC on 2026-10-12. Raspberry Pi names no substitute: it is the only polarity-marked 0806 part. Fit it with the dot on pad 2 (DVDD, silkscreen dot), the end away from the top edge; the core has no supply until it is on. Gerbers unchanged. |

Firmware for proto2: set SD_PWR_EN (expander port 0 bit 6) after the detect switch reads a card and before any SPI; return the SD pins to inputs before clearing it.

Sheets are generated by `scripts/gen_<sheet>.py` on top of `scripts/schlib.py`, netlist-style (labels on every pin, no wires). Regenerating a sheet overwrites hand edits, so once a sheet is being edited in KiCad, retire its generator. Spec: [../docs/v3-architecture.md](../docs/v3-architecture.md).

## Edge connector geometry

Measured from the v2.2 fab Gerbers (`../old/tsPico2_2Fab/Gerber/tsPico.GTL`, `.GBL`, `.Outline`):

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Board | 130.00 x 64.25 mm, 1.6 mm (v2.2 was 100 mm tall; v3 body is 56 mm plus the 8.25 mm tongue) |
| Body | 130.00 x 64.00 mm |
| Tongue | 83.66 mm wide, 8.25 mm deep, left edge 18.48 mm from the board's left edge, 1.0 mm corner chamfers |
| Positions | 32 at 2.54 mm pitch, 31 fingers per side |
| Keying slot | position 6, 2.03 mm wide, 8.26 mm deep |
| Fingers | 1.651 x 8.255 mm (65 x 325 mil), first finger centre 2.52 mm from the tongue's left edge, bottom edge 0.32 mm above the tongue edge |

Pad names are `A1`..`A32` on F.Cu and `B1`..`B32` on B.Cu (no A6/B6), following the TS2068 Technical Manual table 2.4.1-1. Position 1 is at the left when the board is viewed from the top with the connector at the bottom.

**Assumption to verify before ordering:** side A (GND, EAR, D7, ..., SOUND) is on the top (component) side and side B (+15V, +5V, address bus, video) on the bottom. This matches how the v2.2 layout routes +5V (bottom trace from the fourth finger), but confirm it against the v2.2 CircuitStudio PCB or a physical card.

Fab notes for the fingers: hard gold over the fingers if the fab offers it (ENIG otherwise), 30 degree bevel on the tongue edge, no copper within 0.3 mm of the slot.

## Regenerating the BOM

```bash
cd hardware
KC=/Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/MacOS/kicad-cli
$KC sch export bom --output bom/tspico-v3-jlcpcb-bom.csv --fields "Value,Reference,Footprint,LCSC" --labels "Comment,Designator,Footprint,LCSC Part #" --group-by "Value,Footprint" --exclude-dnp tspico-v3.kicad_sch
```

Part choices are priced against JLCPCB's catalog with `python3 scripts/jlc_price.py --boards 10`; the reasoning for each substitution is in `docs/bom-cost-optimization.md` (2026-09-17: parts + fees per board went from $32.95 to $14.93 on a 10-board run, to $14.48 after the DMA option was dropped; fitted logic packages from 16 to 7). The board was laid out again for these parts on 2026-09-17 (see the routing section).

## Fab outputs

`python3 scripts/gen_fab.py` writes `fab/jlcpcb/`: `tspico-v3-gerbers.zip` (4-layer Gerbers + Excellon drill), `tspico-v3-bom.csv` and `tspico-v3-cpl.csv` (JLCPCB assembly format), and refreshes the two BOMs in `bom/`. `fab/NOTES.md` lists what to check on upload (L1 has no LCSC number yet; rotations of QFN/SOT/connector parts need checking in JLC's placement preview; J3 and J5 are through-hole).

## Layout notes

- Body 130 x 56 mm (the register row of the first layout is gone). Left edge, top to bottom: ESP32-C3 antenna, USB-C, micro SD. The TF-01A socket's shell is flush with the board edge (its shield pads sit 3 mm back, so nothing needs edge clearance). Right edge: 3.5 mm jack in the corner, DE-9 below it.
- Top edge: buttons in a 2x2 block on 0.5 in centres (top row TS Reset, NMI; below them RUN, BOOTSEL; proto1/2 had all four in a row), then the OLED header. The module on J3 covers everything right of the buttons, so nothing there may be taller than its standoff. The LED (D4, R5) sits left of the ESP32, between the mounting hole and the USB-C socket. A bottom-layer keepout (no tracks or vias) sits under the RP2350B.
- Row above the fingers: data U3, high address byte U2 directly over fingers A15-A8, control U4 (U3 is turned 180 degrees from the others: its A side is the Z80 side so DIR can be /RD itself). Row above that: low address byte U1 over U2, fed from fingers A0-A3 on the left and A7-A4 on the right along the bottom layer, then strobe OR gates U6 and open-drain drivers U7. Both address buffers put plain bytes on MD0-MD7, so the firmware sees ordinary addresses. I2C expander U15 and the joystick conditioning network sit beside the DE-9; audio and the two LDOs above them.
- RP2350B core regulator copied from "Hardware design with RP2350" figure 4: input cap C22 and output cap C20 (0402) straddle the VREG_LX pin, LX runs 3.7 mm under both to L1, VIN and PGND go straight into C22, feedback senses at C20, the AVDD filter R28/C39 sits above. L1 is the polarised Abracon part: pad 2 (dot) is the DVDD end, away from the top edge of the board.
- Crystal 6.5 mm from its pins with R20 between chip and crystal; XOUT_R runs under the crystal to pad 3. XIN and XOUT drop one pad lane after leaving the chip so DVDD pin 32 has room for its via.
- Before ordering: check the D-sub footprint against the CONNFLY DS1037 drawing, check `lib/tspico.pretty/microSD_TF-01A` and `L_Abracon_AOTA-B201610` against the parts' drawings (both were generated from LCSC's land patterns). (TF-01A card detect: low = card in, confirmed on proto1.)

## Routing flow and state

```bash
cd hardware
PY=/Applications/KiCad/KiCad.app/Contents/Frameworks/Python.framework/Versions/Current/bin/python3
$PY scripts/gen_layout.py     # placement, keepouts, planes (discards all routing)
$PY scripts/preroute.py       # locked hand routes: regulator, crystal, DVDD spine, inner supply links, bottom-row fan
$PY scripts/autoroute.py --jar <freerouting-2.4.1.jar> --passes 100     # about 25 minutes
$PY scripts/finish.py         # widen necked tracks, join orphan supply islands, retry open nets, drop unused vias and stubs
$PY scripts/silk.py           # place reference designators clear of pads, silk and the edge; trim outlines at the edge
```

Or all five in one command, which is the way to run it:

```bash
$PY scripts/flow.py --jar <freerouting-2.4.1.jar>      # 10 to 15 minutes per attempt, up to 3 attempts
```

Freerouting does not give the same result twice from the same input. Before U1's address pins had their pre-routed via fan (below), about one run in three left one of them (A3, A5) sealed in behind its neighbours' vias; with the fan, three runs out of three closed on the first attempt. The safety net stays: if a signal net is left open, `finish.py` tries lifting the neighbours' copper around the pin, routing the open net and putting the neighbours back, but refuses a result that needs more than three vias per net (the only way out of such a pocket has been a 9-via, 70 mm detour). If the board still is not clean `finish.py` exits non-zero and `flow.py` routes again from the placement, keeping the failed board as `fab/route/attempt-N.kicad_pcb`.

Rules: 0.15 mm signal tracks (the router may neck down to 0.11 mm in a squeeze; board minimum 0.1 mm, JLCPCB's is 0.09), 0.4 mm on the 5 V rails and +3V3_WIFI, 0.127 mm clearance, every via 0.6/0.3 mm (JLCPCB's no-surcharge size; the ESP32 footprint's 0.2 mm thermal vias are opened to 0.3 mm by `gen_layout.py`). The pre-stitch step skips the RP2350B's pins: a via 0.9 mm out of a 0.4 mm pitch pin blocks both neighbours' escape lanes. Its exposed pad gets five ground vias.

What `preroute.py` fixes in place before the autorouter runs, because the router handles a fully used 0.4 mm pitch QFN badly (whichever pins it leaves for last end up sealed behind its own vias):

- the regulator and crystal, as above;
- the **DVDD spine**: 0.25 mm track under the package from the regulator's pin 65 to DVDD pins 51, 10 and 32 (10, 15 and 22 mm from the output capacitor; only the first 3 mm at the pins is 0.15 mm), and pin 51's 100 nF 2 mm from its pin;
- the 3.3 V supply pins linked in groups under the package, each group with one via: 50 to corner pin 41, 68/69 to 76 to pin 5, 24 to 29;
- the **bottom pad row** (pins 1-20, all used) as a deterministic fan: straight out to a staggered depth, a 45 degree turn, and a via on a common line at 0.75 mm spacing, which is the tightest legal pattern for 0.6 mm vias at 0.4 mm pitch.
- **U1's Z80-side pins** (A0-A7): each runs straight in under U1's body to its own via, in two staggered rows 1.26 and 2.06 mm from the pads. U1's bottom pad row faces U2's top row across a 0.8 mm gap, so these pins can only leave under U1, and their fingers are on the bottom layer, so each needs a via anyway. Neighbouring vias are 1.03 mm apart, and a track on either layer still passes between two vias of the near row.

State on 2026-09-17 (DMA option dropped, address buffers delivering plain bytes, audio and joystick passives respaced for silkscreen, U1 address fan pre-routed): **fully routed, DRC clean at every severity (no errors, no warnings, no unconnected items)**, no tracks on either plane layer. 2073 track segments, 378 vias, all 0.6/0.3 mm, no track under 0.13 mm. Produced by `flow.py` on its first attempt with no hand edits. (Run DRC on the board where it sits: a copy outside the project folder is checked against KiCad's default rules and reports hundreds of false clearance errors.)

Silkscreen: `silk.py` sets designators to 0.8 mm text and tries positions around each part until one clears pads, other silk, other parts and the board edge; rows of equal parts all get the label on the same side (above a flat part, to the right of an upright one), and a hidden designator gets a second chance by moving a neighbour's. The layout leaves room for this: passive rows are 3.2 mm apart in the audio block, 2.7 mm in the SD pull-ups, and the joystick RC columns are 2.6 mm apart. 139 of 140 designators are on silk; only C39 (the VREG_AVDD capacitor, boxed in by the design guide's regulator layout) is hidden, and it is on F.Fab with all the others. Outlines of edge connectors that overhang the board are trimmed where they cross the edge.

Rerunning `autoroute.py` continues from the current routing (it exports existing tracks); `--fresh` starts over. Do not rerun `gen_layout.py`: it rebuilds the board from placement and discards routing.
